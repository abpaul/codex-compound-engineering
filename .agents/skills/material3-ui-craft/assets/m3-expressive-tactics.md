# M3 Expressive (tactics + usability guardrails)

Use M3 Expressive as *tactics* you can turn up/down per surface. The goal is to increase emotional impact and steer attention **without** sacrificing clarity, familiarity, or accessibility.

## Fundamentals (attention steering)

Expressive design typically uses:

- **Shape** (silhouette, corner radii, decorative masks)
- **Color/contrast** (role contrast + surface tones for hierarchy)
- **Typography emphasis** (editorial moments; emphasized styles for key actions/info)
- **Containment** (grouping + bright/large containers for what matters)
- **Motion** (spring-like physics, micro-animations, shape morph)
- **Component flexibility** (controls adapt to context + screen size)

## Research signals (why it works)

When applied well, expressive tactics tend to:

- Increase preference across ages (often strongest with younger users).
- Score higher on emotional attributes (playful, energetic, creative, friendly).
- Improve usability by steering attention to key UI elements (including faster discovery in lab studies).
- Help accessibility by making targets larger and containment/contrast clearer.
- Require restraint: context still matters (high-trust flows often need calmer intensity).

## Tactics (pick 2–3 per surface)

1. **Use a variety of shapes**
   - Use shape contrast to direct focus (mix classic + abstract silhouettes).
   - Caution: overly small shapes can make essential actions feel less important.

2. **Apply rich, nuanced colors**
   - Use contrast between `primary/secondary/tertiary` roles and **surface tones** to create hierarchy.
   - Caution: without contrast, elements blend together and actions become harder to find.

3. **Guide attention with typography**
   - Use emphasized styles (weight/size/spacing/color) for headlines and key actions.
   - Add “editorial” moments sparingly; keep body text readable and calm.

4. **Contain content for emphasis**
   - Group related content into logical containers; give critical content more space and brighter surface mapping.
   - Caution: ungrouped information becomes visually flat and harder to scan.

5. **Add fluid, natural motion**
   - Use shape morph/surface effects to make interactions feel alive.
   - Motion must be fast, predictable, and respectful of reduced-motion preferences.

6. **Leverage component flexibility**
   - Adapt controls to context (environment, input method, screen size).
   - Prefer canonical layouts for large/foldable screens; customize only where it improves completion.

7. **Combine tactics to create hero moments**
   - Hero moments are brief, delightful, and unexpected. Limit to **1–2 per product**.
   - Identify hero moments by asking:
     - Is the interaction emotionally impactful?
     - Is this a key interaction where extra clarity or emphasis helps?

## Usability posture (non-negotiables)

- Start from **primary goals**. Prefer one dominant primary action per screen; keep secondary/tertiary goals discoverable but quieter.
- Don’t stack every tactic at once: too many emphases increases cognitive load.
- Respect established patterns and labeling; don’t remove labels if it harms recognition.
- Prioritize **functionality + accessibility** over visual flourish (focus visibility, contrast, target sizes, reduced-motion).
- Test and iterate: validate that key actions are found faster and errors drop.
